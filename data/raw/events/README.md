# `events.csv` — data dictionary and provenance  (Stage 1b)

Generated by `src/data/fetch_events.py`; run summary in `events_meta.json`;
venue screening in `venues.csv`. **Do not hand-edit** — rerun

```bash
python -m src.data.fetch_events --config configs/default.yaml
```

95 events × 22 columns, 2017-01-01 → 2017-06-30, at 4 venues.

**Venue-level facts live in `venues.csv`, one row per venue, joined on `venue_name`** —
coordinates, capacity, nearest sensor, sensor counts, the OSM coordinate check.
They are not repeated on the event rows.

---

## 1. Time zone — Pacific, not Eastern

`start_time` / `end_time` are **local wall-clock in `America/Los_Angeles`
(US Pacific)**. They are *not* US Eastern and *not* UTC.

This is forced, not chosen: PEMS-BAY's `speed.csv` column labels are local
wall-clock Pacific, and `align.py` joins events to traffic **by label**. Any
other convention shifts every event by 3 h (Eastern) or 7–8 h (UTC).

Consequences that are already handled:

* `start_time_utc` carries the same instant in UTC, for verification only.
  Nothing downstream reads it. Offset is −08:00 (PST) before 2017-03-12 and
  −07:00 (PDT) after — visible in the file: `2017-01-01 13:25 → 21:25 UTC`
  vs `2017-03-17 19:00 → 2017-03-18 02:00 UTC`.
* ESPN publishes kickoffs in UTC; the conversion to Pacific happens once, in
  `collect_espn`, and never again.
* 2017-03-12 02:00–02:59 **does not exist** locally (DST spring-forward). No
  event falls in it; the two events that day (13:15 and 19:00) are both after
  the transition.

## 2. Which sensor? — `nearest_sensor_*`

Distances are haversine, from the venue to every sensor in
`data/raw/augmented-pems-bay/data/sensor_graph/sensor_metadata.csv` — the same
file `align.py` uses to place graph nodes, so these are the distances the model
actually sees.

| Venue | Nearest sensor | Freeway | Distance | Sensors ≤2 km | ≤5 km | Events |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SAP Center | **402057** | SR-87 S | 0.51 km | 23 | 107 | 69 |
| Avaya Stadium | **400508** | I-880 S | 0.60 km | 15 | 88 | 9 |
| Shoreline Amphitheatre | **401507** | US-101 N | 1.56 km | 4 | 22 | 12 |
| Levi's Stadium | **400973** | SR-237 E | 1.63 km | 4 | 43 | 5 |
| Stanford Stadium | 401507 | US-101 N | 7.59 km | 0 | 0 | **0** |
| Oracle Arena | 401507 | US-101 N | 39.09 km | 0 | 0 | dropped |
| Oakland Coliseum | 401507 | US-101 N | 39.17 km | 0 | 0 | dropped |
| AT&T Park | 401507 | US-101 N | 48.99 km | 0 | 0 | dropped |

`nearest_sensor_id` names *one* sensor, but the event feature is **not**
attached to it. `event_load[t,n]` reaches **every** node with a decay
`exp(−d/4 km)` (`features.event_load.tau_km`). The nearest sensor is the
identity of the node that receives the strongest signal; the `sensors_within_*`
counts say how many nodes can plausibly move at all. Levi's has only 4 sensors
within 2 km, so even U2 can light up few nodes strongly.

## 3. Why these venues — the inclusion rule

**Rule: keep a venue iff its nearest sensor is ≤ 15 km**
(`events.max_venue_distance_km`). Applied mechanically to all 7 registered
venues; the 4 rejected ones stay in `venues.csv` with `included=no` and an
`exclusion_reason`, so the screening is auditable rather than implicit.

Oracle Arena / Oakland Coliseum / AT&T Park sit 39–49 km out, across the bay.
They cannot move South Bay traffic, and in the previous hand-made file they
were **75.8 % of all rows** — pure noise in a globally-broadcast event channel.

**The 15 km is not tuned, and the result is not sensitive to it.** The
registered venues sort as 0.51, 0.60, 1.63, 7.59 km — and then nothing until
39.09 km. That is a **31.5 km empty gap**, so *any* threshold in (7.6, 39.1] —
10, 15, 20, 30 — keeps the same 4 venues and produces a byte-identical
`events.csv`. 15 km is just the middle of the gap. The threshold separates two
well-separated clusters; it does not draw a fine line, and no result depends on
where inside the gap it is placed.

The `exp(−d/4 km)` decay in `event_load` would suppress the far venues on its
own in any case: at 39 km the weight is 5.7e-05, i.e. **6e-05 of SAP Center's**.
The threshold is belt-and-braces, and it exists mainly so the exclusion is
explicit and auditable rather than buried in a decay constant.

Stanford Stadium passes the rule (7.59 km) but hosts **0** events in
January–June (college football is autumn). `align.py` therefore excludes it
from `dist_to_venue` as well — measuring distance to a venue that never fires
would tell the model a node is "near an event venue" while that node never sees
an event. It is kept in the registry so the zero is explicit.

## 4. Why these events — the enumeration rule

**Enumerate venue calendars, not teams.** Enumerating the 5 pro teams collects
27 Sharks games and misses the 26 AHL Barracuda games, 15 concerts, 2 wrestling
cards and an ice show that SAP Center also hosted — and misses U2 at Levi's
entirely. Two complementary sources, in priority order:

0. **Shoreline Amphitheatre** deserves a note: it is an outdoor amphitheatre whose
   season runs May–October, so all 12 in-period shows fall in
   **2017-05-26 … 06-25**. Under the `single` split that is the test window, which
   held 12 events in total before this venue was added and holds 24 after it — the
   count doubles. A venue list built around the winter sports calendar could not
   see it, which is why it was missed until 2026-09-02.

1. **ESPN scoreboard API** — `site.api.espn.com/.../scoreboard?dates=YYYYMMDD`,
   one request per day per league (MLB/NHL/NBA/MLS/NFL), 181 days. Exact UTC
   start time and the announced attendance. Preseason excluded
   (`include_preseason: false`).
2. **Wayback Machine snapshots of the venues' own calendars** —
   `sapcenter.com/events/all` and `levisstadium.com/events*`, plus each SAP
   event's own detail page for the published show time. Covers everything the
   leagues don't.
3. **Shoreline Amphitheatre's listing page** (`mountainviewamphitheater.com/events`),
   which publishes schema.org **JSON-LD** `MusicEvent` blocks. The `startDate`
   **date** is authoritative; the **time** is not — every block reads 19:00–23:00
   with a nonsense `+02:00` offset, i.e. a WordPress plugin default. Only the date
   is taken; the start falls back to the per-type convention and is flagged
   `start_time_is_estimated=1`. Season-pass products ("Megaticket",
   "Ticket to Rock") are dropped — they are ticket bundles, not events, and would
   double-count the shows they cover.

On a venue+day collision ESPN wins (it has the exact time). Two events at the
same venue on the same day are both kept only if their starts differ by >3 h —
that is how the 4 genuine matinee/evening double-headers survive.

**Audited corrections (`EXCLUSIONS` / `MANUAL_ROWS` in `fetch_events.py`).** The
venue listing is not ground truth, and two cases where an independent source
contradicts it are recorded in code rather than hand-edited into the CSV:

* **4 rows removed** — "Barracuda vs. Tucson" on 2017-04-11/12/13/14. The club's
  own site (`sjbarracuda.com`, archived) shows April 2017 as Apr 1 vs Manitoba,
  Apr 8 *at* Stockton, Apr 9 vs Stockton, Apr 15 *at* Bakersfield, Apr 21 vs
  Stockton; Tucson appears nowhere in April, and its front page on 2017-04-22
  reads "Last Game Sat, Apr 15". The AHL also does not schedule four consecutive
  home dates against one opponent. These were stale SAP listing entries left
  after a schedule change (two from the 2016-10 snapshot, two from 2017-02).
* **2 rows added** — Barracuda Calder Cup playoff home games on 2017-04-21 19:00
  and 2017-04-23 15:00, printed on the club's front page but absent from every
  archived SAP snapshot. `time_source = venue_site`,
  `date_source = team_site_verified`.

**Known integrity guard:** SAP's URL slugs are unique only per *title*, so a
4-game home series against one opponent yields `barracuda-vs-tucson`,
`-tucson-1`, `-tucson-2`… and the listing page can link the wrong one.
`parse_sap_detail` now also reads the date the detail page itself prints and
**rejects the time when it disagrees** with the listing date. 3 pages were
rejected this way (2 in the study period: 2017-04-11 and 2017-04-13 Barracuda
vs. Tucson); those rows fall back to the convention time and are flagged
`start_time_is_estimated=1`.

## 5. Estimated vs. observed — read these three flags

The three time/size fields are sourced **independently**. Never assume a row is
wholly observed or wholly assumed.

| Field | Flag | Observed | Estimated |
|---|---|---|---|
| `start_time` | `start_time_is_estimated` | 79 | **16** |
| `end_time` | `end_time_is_estimated` | 0 | **95 (all)** |
| `expected_attendance` | `attendance_is_estimated` | **41** | **54** |

* **`start_time`** — `time_source` ∈
  `espn` (37, exact published kickoff) ·
  `venue_detail_page` (40, the venue's own published show time) ·
  `venue_site` (2, the club's own front page — the two playoff additions) ·
  `venue_convention` (**16, ASSUMED** = `events.defaults[type].start`).
  The 16 assumed are the 4 Levi's events (Bacon and Beer Classic, Monster Jam,
  **U2**, Charlie Wedemeyer) plus all 12 Shoreline shows, whose listing publishes
  a usable date but only a plugin-default time.
* **`end_time`** — `duration_source` is `nominal_by_type` for **every** row:
  `start + events.defaults[type].duration_min`. **No source publishes a finish
  time.** The egress spike happens *after* it, so eval windows must extend past
  `end_time` (`eval.event_phases.egress: [0, 90]`).
* **`expected_attendance`** — `attendance_kind` is the field to read, not
  `attendance_source`:
  * **`boxscore_observed` (4) — the strongest figures in the file.** Real,
    varying, reported crowd counts from **Billboard Boxscore** (the industry
    figure promoters report), transcribed into Wikipedia tour-date tables that
    cite `billboard.com/biz/current-boxscore`:

    | Date | Venue | Event | Sold / offered | Load |
    |---|---|---|---|---|
    | 2017-05-17 | Levi's Stadium | U2: The Joshua Tree Tour | **50,072 / 50,072** | 100% |
    | 2017-06-02 | Shoreline | Chris Stapleton | **17,305 / 22,000** | 79% |
    | 2017-06-03 | Shoreline | Dead And Company | **21,780 / 22,015** | 99% |
    | 2017-06-04 | Shoreline | Dead And Company | **16,422 / 21,949** | 75% |

    Two things these numbers show that no default can. **A stadium concert sells
    a much smaller house than the sport does**: U2 offered 50,072 seats at
    Levi's, not its 68,500 NFL capacity — the stage eats one end. The
    `stadium_concert` default of 55,000 was therefore **9.8% too high**, and is
    now overridden by the real figure. And **the same band on consecutive nights
    differed by 33%** (21,780 → 16,422), which a per-type constant cannot
    express at all.

    Coverage is 4 of the 58 rows that would otherwise use a default, because it
    needs both a Wikipedia tour page *and* an editor who transcribed the Boxscore
    row. Searched and not found: Lady Antebellum, Live 105's BFD, Future/Migos
    (×2), Boston, Brad Paisley, Korn, ID10T (×2).
  * `announced_observed` (11) — a real per-game announced figure that varies
    (e.g. 70,178 for SEA @ SF; Sharks games 17,386–17,515).
  * `announced_sellout_at_capacity` (26) — published by ESPN, but exactly equal
    to the venue's capacity for that configuration: all 9 Avaya MLS matches at
    18,000 and 17 sold-out Sharks games at 17,562. These are **right-censored**,
    not mismeasured: the figure means "demand ≥ capacity", and the true value
    above the ceiling is unknowable. The measured shape says so — at SAP Center
    the 10 non-sellout games produce **10 distinct values** (17,386, 17,387,
    17,402 … 17,515), the signature of a real varying quantity, while 17,562
    occurs **17 times** as a point mass exactly at the ceiling, and nothing ever
    exceeds it. So these 26 rows carry no information *about each other*.

    **Note what this is NOT.** It is not "announced vs. counted". *None* of the
    37 ESPN figures are turnstile counts — US pro leagues report tickets
    sold/distributed, and the NFL row proves it: **70,178 at Levi's exceeds its
    68,500 seating capacity**. You cannot seat more people than you have seats.
    All 37 therefore overstate the crowd that actually travelled, by the no-show
    rate. The distinction here is censoring, not measurement quality.
  * `type_default_estimate` (54) — **ASSUMED**, `events.defaults[type].attendance`.
    Those defaults are not one-off guesses; two rules are applied uniformly.
    **League types** sit at or just under the home venue's capacity, because
    those fixtures routinely sell out (NHL 17,400/17,562 = 0.991; MLS
    18,000/18,000 = 1.000; NFL 68,500/68,500 = 1.000) — and in practice they are
    almost never used, since 37/37 league rows carry ESPN's own figure.
    **Concert types** sit at **0.80 × venue capacity** (concert 15,352/19,190 =
    0.800; stadium_concert 40,058/50,072 = 0.800; amphitheater_concert
    18,000/22,500 = 0.800). The 0.80 factor was a convention, not a source — but
    it is now **partly validated**: the three Boxscore-measured Shoreline shows
    load at 0.79, 0.99 and 0.75 (mean **0.84**, mean crowd **18,502** against the
    18,000 default, 2.7% low). The `stadium_concert` default did **not** hold up:
    it took 0.80 of the *NFL* capacity, but a stadium concert offers far fewer
    seats, and the real U2 figure is 9.8% below the default. **AHL 4,412 is the exception, and the
    only default with a measured basis**: it is the San Jose Barracuda's
    published season average, 24th of 30 in the AHL (hockeydb, via the club's
    Wikipedia article). It is the **2015-16** figure — one season before the
    study period — because no 2016-17 number is published anywhere reachable
    (hockeydb renders via JS, the AHL season page's attendance field is empty,
    the club site does not report gates). It replaced an unsourced 4,200, which
    turned out to be **the seat count of the arena the Barracuda moved to in
    2022**, not an attendance figure at all. Low, but correctly low: they play in
    the Sharks' 17,562-seat building, curtained down, in a market the parent club
    already fills — 4,412/17,562 = 25%.
    Covers all 24 AHL (4,412), 15 arena concerts (15,352), 10 amphitheatre
    concerts (18,000), 3 festivals (15,000), 2 wrestling (12,000), ice show
    (9,000), motorsport (40,000), amateur sport (10,000). `stadium_concert` no
    longer appears here — its one row carries a real Boxscore figure.

`confidence` rolls the two *variable* flags up: `high` (37) = both observed ·
`medium` (46) = one assumed · `low` (12) = both assumed. `end_time` is excluded
from it because it is assumed everywhere and would flatten the column.

**For the paper:** "announced attendance" is only defensible for the 11
`announced_observed` rows. State that 26 more are sellout figures pinned to
capacity and 48 are per-type estimates.

## 6. Column reference

**Origin taxonomy.** "Derived" was too coarse a word — it lumped a haversine
distance (which cannot be wrong unless its inputs are) together with `end_time`
(which rests entirely on a duration nobody published). Four categories, and only
one of them means the value is uncertain:

| Origin | Meaning | Can it be wrong? |
|---|---|---|
| `fetched` | read from a source document | only if the source is wrong — `source_url` retrieves it |
| `computed` | deterministic function of other columns | no, unless its inputs are |
| **`assumed`** | rests on a `configs/*.yaml` value **no source published** | **yes — this is the uncertain category** |
| **`hand_entered`** | typed into the `VENUES` registry in `fetch_events.py` | **not in principle, but NO script verifies it** |

The same taxonomy is emitted machine-readably as `column_origins` in
`events_meta.json`.

| # | Column | Origin | Meaning / note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `event_id` | computed | `venue_slug_YYYYMMDDTHHMM` |
| 2 | `start_time` | fetched (79) / **assumed (16)** | local Pacific wall-clock; read `start_time_basis` |
| 3 | `end_time` | **assumed (95 — all)** | `start + events.defaults[type].duration_min` |
| 4 | `timezone` | computed | constant, from `data.timezone`. Kept despite being constant: reading these as Eastern or UTC silently destroys every event window |
| 5 | `venue_name` | fetched | **the join key into `venues.csv`** |
| 6 | `expected_attendance` | fetched (41) / **assumed (54)** | read `attendance_kind` |
| 7 | `attendance_kind` | computed | boxscore / observed / sellout-at-capacity / estimate |
| 8 | `event_type` | fetched or computed | the league name, or `classify()` on the title |
| 9 | `event_name` | fetched | title as published |
| 10 | `duration_min` | **assumed** | `events.defaults[type].duration_min` |
| 11 | `date_source` | computed | label; the DATE itself is fetched on **every** row |
| 12 | `time_source` | computed | label |
| 13 | `attendance_source` | computed | label |
| 14 | `duration_source` | computed | label; constant `nominal_by_type` |
| 15 | `start_time_basis` | computed | `exact_published` (79) / `type_convention` (16) |
| 16 | `assumed_from` | computed | **the exact config keys and values this row leans on** |
| 17 | `start_time_is_estimated` | computed | flag over `time_source` |
| 18 | `end_time_is_estimated` | computed | flag; 1 on every row |
| 19 | `attendance_is_estimated` | computed | flag over `attendance_source` |
| 20 | `confidence` | computed | rollup of the two variable flags |
| 21 | `source` | computed | provenance tag incl. Wayback timestamp / Boxscore citation |
| 22 | `source_url` | computed | **retrievable URL** — refetch and check any row |

### Three further errors found and fixed on 2026-09-02

**1. `stadium_concert` was derived from a capacity that does not exist.** It was
`0.80 × 68,500` (the *NFL* capacity) = 55,000, then briefly `0.80 × 50,072` =
40,058. Both are wrong. A stadium concert has **no stable capacity** — it depends
where the stage goes: U2 offered 50,072 in 2017, while the venue's record is
80,058 (Ed Sheeran, 2023). Taking 0.80 of a house that was already sold out
double-discounts it. The default is now the **measured** 50,072 (U2's Boxscore),
in the same class as AHL 4,412, and Levi's is deliberately given **no** concert
entry in `CAPACITY_BY_USE`.

**2. `Live 105's BFD 2017` was billed as a 3-hour evening concert.** It is an
all-day festival. KITS' own history: *"BFD was held every June at the Shoreline
Amphitheatre … that would **run all day**, with up-and-coming bands performing on
the festival stage **during the day**, and more established bands on the main
stage **at night**."* `classify()` missed it because the title carries no
festival keyword.

**3. `festival` conflated two different traffic shapes.** A food expo
(Bacon and Beer Classic, 11:00–17:00) and an all-day music festival are not the
same event: the latter spreads arrivals across the afternoon and empties near
midnight. A new type **`music_festival`** (12:00, 660 min) now covers BFD and
both ID10T days — 3 rows moved. The hours are a **convention**, not a published
schedule, and all 3 rows stay `start_time_is_estimated=1`.

**Checked and NOT an error:** Future/Migos appears twice at Shoreline, on 06-13
and 06-15. That is real — the Nobody Safe Tour's own date table runs
06-13 Mountain View → 06-14 Wheatland → 06-15 Mountain View.

A full sweep of the remaining 95 rows found nothing else: no start time outside
06:00–21:30, no cross-midnight row, no duplicate (venue, start_time), no two
events overlapping *at the same venue*, and every non-league title consistent
with its assigned type.

### The 24 hand-entered numbers — 16 are now verified, 8 are not

`venue_lat` / `venue_lon` / `venue_capacity` for 8 venues are typed into the
`VENUES` registry rather than fetched. A fetched value that is wrong gets caught
by `source_url`; a typed one does not — unless something checks it. The 16
coordinates now are checked, by `verify_venue_coords()`, against **OpenStreetMap
Nominatim** (one cached query per venue, so reruns are offline and
deterministic). The run **asserts** and refuses to write if any venue is more
than 500 m off.

| Venue | Δ from OSM |
|---|---|
| Oakland Coliseum | 12 m |
| Oracle Arena / AT&T Park | 15 m |
| Shoreline Amphitheatre | 18 m |
| SAP Center | 27 m |
| Levi's Stadium | 41 m |
| Avaya Stadium | 52 m |
| Stanford Stadium | 96 m |

All 8 within 100 m — smaller than the buildings themselves, and negligible
against `tau_km = 4.0` (a 100 m error moves the decay weight by 2.5%). The
per-row value is in `venue_coord_osm_delta_m`; the queries and raw results are in
`venues.csv` and `events_meta.json → venue_coord_check`.

**The 8 capacities were checked on 2026-09-02** against each venue's Wikipedia
infobox. Six matched exactly: Avaya/PayPal Park 18,000 · Levi's 68,500 ·
Shoreline 22,500 · Stanford 50,424 · Oracle Arena 19,596 · AT&T Park 41,915
("41,915 (2007–2019)", i.e. the 2017 configuration). But the check found a real
error:

**A venue does not have one capacity — it has one per configuration.**
SAP Center seats **17,562 for hockey but 19,190 for a concert** (no ice, floor
seating), 18,300 for wrestling, 18,543 for basketball. The single hand-entered
17,562 was applied to all 69 SAP events, including the 15 concerts. Both
consequences are now fixed:

* the `concert` attendance default was `0.80 × 17,562 = 14,000`; it is now
  `0.80 × 19,190 = **15,352**` — the old value was ~9% low on **15 rows**;
* the same error at Levi's had already been caught empirically: the
  `stadium_concert` default took `0.80 × 68,500` (the *NFL* capacity) = 55,000,
  but a concert puts the stage in an end zone and U2's own Boxscore shows
  **50,072 offered**. It is now `0.80 × 50,072 = **40,058**`.

`CAPACITY_BY_USE` and `TYPE_TO_USE` in `fetch_events.py` now hold a capacity per
configuration and map each event type onto one; `venue_capacity(venue, type)`
resolves it. Both are echoed into `events_meta.json`
(`venues[*].capacity_by_use`, `capacity_check`). Oakland Coliseum was corrected
to the cited 46,847 (baseball) — an excluded venue with 0 events, so it affects
only the screening log.

### What each `time_source` value is actually backed by (measured 2026-09-02)

| `time_source` | n | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| `espn` | 37 | **37/37 carry a real UTC kickoff.** Local times spread 13:00 ×2, 13:25 ×1, 17:30 ×1, 18:00 ×1, 19:00 ×8, 19:30 ×23, 20:00 ×1 — the shape of a real schedule. **0 placeholders** (no midnight/TBD values). |
| `venue_detail_page` | 40 | The page prints it literally: `Event Times · Friday, March 17, 2017 · 7:00 PM`. `parse_sap_detail` has a riskier fallback path ("first time that is not the doors time"), but it fired on only **3 pages, all outside the study period** — every in-period row used the explicit `Event Times` match **and** passed the date guard. |
| `venue_site` | 2 | The club's own front page on 2017-04-22: "Home Fri, Apr 21 **7:00PM** · Home Sun, Apr 23 **3:00PM**". |
| `venue_convention` | **16** | **Nothing.** `events.defaults[type].start`. 4 Levi's events + all 12 Shoreline shows. |

**Why Shoreline's published time is discarded.** Its JSON-LD does carry a
`startDate` time, but **all 127 blocks read `19:00`–`23:00` with a `+02:00`
offset** — an impossible offset for California, and identical for daytime
festivals and evening concerts alike. It is a WordPress plugin constant, not
per-event data. Only the date is taken.

**Can the real Shoreline times be recovered? No — checked and ruled out
(2026-09-02).** Unlike SAP Center, whose per-event pages print
`Event Times · … · 7:00 PM`, Shoreline's per-event pages carry **no time at
all**: four were fetched and parsed (Dead And Company, Korn & Stone Sour, ID10T
Day 1, Live 105's BFD) and **zero time patterns and zero "Doors" strings** were
found in any of them — they are prose ticket-sales pages. Live Nation's own
venue pages are not in the Wayback CDX for this period. So these 12 rows stay
`venue_convention`, and there is no cheaper source to try.

## 7. Known limitations

* **54 of 95 attendances are per-type constants**, so within AHL, concerts, etc.
  `event_load` varies only by venue distance and duration, not by draw. Across
  types it does vary 4.2× at SAP Center (4,200 AHL → 17,562 NHL).
* **26 more are sellout figures at capacity** — published, but no variance.
* **All 95 `end_time`s are nominal.** A concert that runs long, or an overtime
  game, is not represented.
* **The study period has no NFL season and no autumn wet season**, so Levi's
  (68,500 seats, 1.63 km) contributes 5 events, only one of them an NFL game.
* **The registry, not the distance threshold, is the binding constraint.**
  Shoreline Amphitheatre was missing until 2026-09-02 and added 12 events; the
  same class of gap may remain. Still unregistered, all inside 15 km:
  CEFCU/Spartan Stadium (1.05 km — checked: SJSU football is Sep–Nov, so
  Jan–Jun holds only a spring exhibition game; negligible), San Jose Civic
  (0.60 km, ~3,000 seats), Mountain Winery (3.47 km, ~2,500 seats) and Stanford's
  Maples Pavilion / Frost Amphitheater (7.3 / 7.9 km). Raising
  `max_venue_distance_km` recovers none of them — only adding them to `VENUES`
  would.
* Wayback coverage is 8 SAP snapshots, 24 Levi's and 7 Shoreline. A one-off event
  that appeared and passed entirely between two snapshots would be missed.
* **A ~87-day hole in SAP snapshot coverage**: none between 2016-11-21 and
  2017-02-16, and the February one only lists events from 02-18 onward. So
  2017-01-01 … 02-17 relies entirely on announcements made before 2016-11-21.
  22 events fall in that span; 12 come from ESPN (fixed league schedules,
  unaffected) and **10 depend on the archived calendar alone**. Anything added to
  the calendar late — a rescheduled show, a comedy date — would be invisible.
